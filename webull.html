Localising brand campaigns for APAC investors

Regional brand execution for a global retail investment platform — adapting campaigns to local audiences, regulatory realities and market maturity.

Organisation
Webull Holdings (Singapore)
My role
Branding Associate
Period
Jul 2022 – Oct 2024
Markets
Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia, Australia

Business context

Webull is a global retail investment platform competing for digitally native investors across multiple markets. Growth in this category depends on brand trust as much as product features: people are being asked to move money into an app, and the decision is emotional as well as rational.

The problem

Brand campaigns built centrally do not transfer cleanly across APAC. Investor maturity, regulatory language around financial promotions, platform mix, competitive intensity and cultural reference points differ market by market. Running the same creative everywhere is cheap and ineffective; rebuilding it everywhere is effective and unaffordable.

Strategic objective

Deliver brand campaigns that stayed recognisably one brand while being credible and compliant in each market, without rebuilding creative from scratch each time.

My precise role

I was a Branding Associate — I executed and localised regional campaigns; I did not set regional brand strategy and I did not hold the budget. On the anniversary campaign, which carried a SGD 1 million allocated budget, I executed the out-of-home advertising and announcement workstreams, coordinating with in-market stakeholders, media agencies and internal technology teams across Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia and Australia.

Stakeholders

In-market stakeholders and country teams across Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia and Australia; external media and creative agencies; internal technology and product teams responsible for the surfaces campaigns ran on; regional brand leadership.

Constraints

  • Regulated category. Financial promotions carry market-specific requirements on claims, risk disclosure and targeting.
  • Speed against consistency. Campaign windows in retail investing are short and often tied to market events.
  • Distributed decision-making. Alignment had to be reached across in-market teams, agencies and technology without direct authority over any of them.

Decisions and trade-offs

Adapt rather than rebuild. The practical trade-off was to standardise brand structure and flex the layers most sensitive to local context — language, references, channel mix and compliance treatment — instead of either forcing one global creative or commissioning bespoke work per market. This kept the brand coherent at the cost of some local optimisation.

Fix the process, not just the asset. Much of the friction was not creative but operational — production and approval cycles running across four markets, several agencies and internal technology teams, each with their own channel. I centralised campaign communications into a single system, on the view that recurring coordination delay costs more over a year than any single campaign's creative quality.

Execution

  • Executed the out-of-home advertising and announcement workstreams of the anniversary campaign, adapting assets for Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia and Australia with in-market stakeholders and media agencies.
  • Coordinated with technology teams on the executional surfaces campaigns depended on.
  • Centralised campaign communications into a single system to cut coordination time across markets, agencies and internal teams.
  • Tracked direct web traffic through the campaign period; supported partnerships, sponsorships and regional events.

Outcome

  • The anniversary campaign ran during a period in which registered users grew 122%. My contribution was the out-of-home workstream and traffic tracking — one input among many into a company-wide campaign, and I would not claim more than that.
  • Coordination time across markets and agencies fell once communications were centralised.

What I learned

Localisation is usually treated as a translation problem and is actually a trust problem. What changes between Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia and Australia is not mainly language — it is which proof a person needs before they will believe a financial brand, and how much regulatory framing they expect to see alongside it. The other lesson was structural: as an associate without authority over agencies, country teams or technology, the only reliable lever was making the process easier for everyone else to say yes to. That is a skill I have used far more since.

Capabilities demonstrated: APAC regional campaign localisation · working with digitally native audiences · agency and vendor coordination · cross-functional influence without authority · marketing in a regulated financial category · process improvement
