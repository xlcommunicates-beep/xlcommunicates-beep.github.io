About

B2B marketing and communications in payments and financial infrastructure, based in Singapore.

Background

I did not take a conventional route into marketing. I spent nearly six years in customer-facing retail while studying advertising and design in the evenings, moved into design and marketing coordination for healthcare and regulated consumer brands, then into brand management and regional campaign work for a retail investment platform across APAC.

The move into financial infrastructure came through fintech. At Teleskop, an early-stage company in digital legacy planning, I owned marketing end to end — positioning, content, campaigns, partnerships and the company's summit programme. At Partior I work on marketing and communications for a blockchain-based clearing and settlement platform backed by DBS, J.P. Morgan, Standard Chartered and Temasek, reporting to the CEO and working across product, commercial, communications, legal, shareholders and partner institutions.

What connects it is a consistent interest in the same problem: something is genuinely valuable and genuinely hard to explain, and the gap between those two facts is costing the business something.

What I am good at

Taking a technical product reality and finding the version of it that a non-specialist decision-maker can hold in their head — without misrepresenting it. That means sitting with engineers and product specialists until I actually understand the mechanics, then making deliberate choices about what to leave out and being able to defend those choices to the people whose work I compressed.

I am also comfortable being the only marketer in the room. Most of my work has been in small teams with wide surface area, where the job is to decide what matters, coordinate people you do not manage, and ship.

What I am working on

Two things, deliberately.

Measurement. Most of my career has been in brand, content and communications where attribution is loose. I can describe what was produced and what was observed; I have not yet owned a marketing-attributed pipeline number. It is the clearest gap in my own practice, and I would rather say so than imply otherwise.

Product depth. I built and run AdvanceBooking, a live multi-tenant SaaS platform with subscription billing on Stripe, largely to stop reasoning about SaaS pricing, onboarding and payments from the outside.

How I work

  • Start from the decision, not the product. Work backwards from what the person has to approve, and what they need to believe in order to approve it.
  • Accuracy is a marketing constraint, not an obstacle. A claim that cannot survive legal review is worthless regardless of how well it converts.
  • Build systems, not one-off assets. A narrative other people can adapt without me is worth more than a perfect deck only I can deliver.
  • Say what is actually known. If a number has no baseline or the attribution is loose, describe what was observed instead of dressing it up.
